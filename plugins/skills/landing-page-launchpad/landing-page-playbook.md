# Landing Page Playbook

How to turn the strategy into a page concept. Read this in Stage 3, Part C.

These are working heuristics from conversion practice. They are well established as defaults, but most of the published evidence is single case studies, and the size of any effect depends on audience, offer and traffic source. Apply them as defaults, do not quote numbers for them, and let the user's knowledge of their audience overrule a default when they give a reason.

## 1. Principles

- **One page, one goal, one action.** Every section either moves the visitor toward the CTA or answers an objection that stands in the way. A section that does neither is cut.
- **The first screen must answer three questions without scrolling:** What is this? Is it for me? What do I do next? A stranger should be able to explain the offer after five seconds.
- **Message match.** The headline confirms what the visitor saw immediately before clicking (strategy: Funnel, "letzter Kontaktpunkt"). A mismatch reads as the wrong page.
- **Phone first.** Traffic from messengers and social media arrives mostly on phones. Design the narrow layout first and widen it afterwards.
- **Specific beats impressive.** A concrete situation, a date, a named outcome persuade; adjectives do not.
- **Ask for little.** Request only the data needed for the next step. Each extra field is a reason to stop.
- **Honesty is part of conversion.** Visitors who feel misled do not show up, unsubscribe, or ask for refunds. The status labels from the strategy decide what the page may claim.

## 2. Section blueprint

Default order. Drop sections that have no real content; do not pad.

| # | Section | Job | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | Orient and offer the action | Headline (outcome for the visitor), subline (for whom, what, how), primary CTA, one trust cue or the key facts (for an event: date, place, price) |
| 2 | Problem | "This is about me" | The typical situation from the strategy, in the audience's words, two to four short beats |
| 3 | Lösung | What changes | The offer in one sentence, then three concrete outcomes; functional and emotional jobs both appear |
| 4 | So läuft es ab | Remove uncertainty | Three to five steps, or the agenda for an event |
| 5 | Für wen / für wen nicht | Qualify | Short lists; saying who it is not for builds credibility |
| 6 | Warum ich / wir | Trust | Provider, relevant experience, photo, real proof |
| 7 | Angebot im Detail | Decide | What is included, date and conditions, price or "kostenlos", what happens after the click |
| 8 | FAQ | Objections | Four to six questions taken from the roast findings and the audience's other challenges |
| 9 | Abschluss-CTA | Act | Promise restated in one line, the same CTA, the same form |
| 10 | Footer | Obligations | Impressum, Datenschutz, contact; no other links |

Navigation: none, or anchor links within the page only. Links that lead away compete with the CTA.

CTA placement: the same action, with the same wording, in the hero, once after the solution or trust section, and at the end. One primary button style on the whole page.

## 4. Copy rules

- Use the audience's words from Stage 1, not the provider's vocabulary. If the user said "abends um zehn noch am Rechner sitzen", that phrase is better than "Work-Life-Balance".
- Headline: the outcome or the relief for the visitor, in under about ten words. Not the product name, not a slogan that needs explaining.
- One form of address throughout (Du or Sie), as decided in Stage 1.
- Short sentences, active verbs, one idea per paragraph, at most three lines per paragraph on a phone.
- Numbers, dates and prices are written exactly as confirmed.
- Button text says what happens: "Platz sichern", "Zur Warteliste", "Termin anfragen". Not "Absenden", not "Mehr erfahren" for the primary action.
- Directly under the button, answer the next worry in a few words (what it costs, what happens to the data, that it is non-binding), if true.
- Avoid filler: "innovativ", "ganzheitlich", "auf das nächste Level", "revolutionär", "maßgeschneidert", "nahtlos", "einzigartig" without saying in what way.
- No fabricated urgency. A deadline or seat limit appears only if it is real, and is stated exactly.

## 5. CTA patterns by type

| Type | Button | Form fields | After the click (in the mockup: success state) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event | "Platz sichern" / "Jetzt anmelden" | Vorname, E-Mail | Confirmation with date, time, place; note that details follow by e-mail |
| Newsletter | "Newsletter abonnieren" | E-Mail | What arrives and how often; note on confirming the address |
| Launch / Warteliste | "Auf die Warteliste" | E-Mail | What they will be told and when |
| Erstgespräch | "Termin anfragen" | Name, E-Mail, optional one-line concern | How and when the reply comes |
| Informationsbroschüre | "Broschüre erhalten" | E-Mail | How it is delivered |

Add a field only when the user named it as a minimum requirement in Stage 1, Block 7.

## 6. Proof without invention

Order of strength, use the strongest that honestly exists:

1. Named testimonial with permission, a specific outcome, ideally a photo
2. Own numbers with a stated basis ("12 Teilnehmende beim ersten Durchgang")
3. Relevant experience and qualifications of the provider, stated plainly
4. The provider's own story of the problem, marked as personal experience
5. A transparent statement that this is new ("Erster Durchgang, deshalb …") together with a low-risk offer

A first-time offer without customers uses 3 to 5. That is weaker than testimonials and far better than invented ones. If proof is missing, reserve the place in the concept under "Fehlt noch" instead of filling it.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- Hero with a slogan and a decorative image that says nothing about the offer
- Several different CTAs competing (demo, newsletter and contact at once)
- A features list where the visitor's situation should be
- Stock photos of anonymous smiling people presented as customers
- Generic claims such as "Hunderte zufriedene Kunden" without a basis
- Key facts for an event (date, place, price) hidden below the first screen
- Long forms, or asking for a phone number without a reason
- Text over busy images with poor contrast
- Sections added because landing pages usually have them (pricing table, logo wall) when there is nothing real to put in

## 8. Examples

All from an unrelated field. They show the pattern; do not reuse them.

| Element | Weak | Strong | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline | „Willkommen bei Praxisglück" | „Abrechnung in zwei Stunden im Monat statt an vier Abenden" | Outcome for the visitor instead of a name. The strong version is only allowed if the strategy lists the figures as `[belegt]` or as the provider's own experience |
| Headline | „Die Zukunft der Praxisverwaltung beginnt jetzt" | „Feierabend um sechs, auch am Monatsende" | A slogan could sit on any page; the strong one names this audience's relief |
| Subline | „Innovative, ganzheitliche Lösungen für Ihren Erfolg" | „Für Physiotherapie-Praxen mit bis zu fünf Angestellten. Ich richte alles bei dir am Empfang ein." | For whom, what, how |
| Button | „Absenden" / „Mehr erfahren" | „Platz sichern" | Says what happens |
| Line under the button | (nothing) | „Kostenlos. Du bekommst eine Bestätigung per E-Mail." | Answers the next worry, and is true |
| Problem section | „In der heutigen schnelllebigen Zeit stehen Praxen vor immer größeren Herausforderungen." | „Es ist der 28., kurz vor acht. Die letzte Patientin ist weg, der Stapel Verordnungen liegt noch da." | The audience's situation in their own words |
| Proof | „Hunderte zufriedene Kunden vertrauen uns" | „Erster Durchgang im Mai mit 8 Praxen; 6 arbeiten weiter damit." | Checkable and specific |
| FAQ | „Warum sind wir die beste Wahl?" | „Was, wenn ich am Termin doch nicht kann?" | A real objection, not a compliment to oneself |

Phrases that mark German copy as machine-written. Rewrite any sentence that uses them:

- openers such as „Stell dir vor, …", „In der heutigen Zeit …", „Kennst du das? …" stacked one after another
- series of „Nicht nur …, sondern auch …" and „Kein X. Kein Y. Nur Z."
- adjectives in threes („schnell, einfach und effektiv")
- imperatives without content: „Entdecke", „Tauche ein", „Starte durch", „Hebe dein Business auf das nächste Level"
- emoji as bullet points, exclamation marks in headings
- a closing line that repeats the paragraph as a slogan

## 9. Guardrails

- The page argues one thing. If a section does not move toward the CTA or answer an objection, cut it instead of polishing it.
- Every factual sentence in the copy is traceable to "Was auf die Seite darf" in the strategy. If it is not there, it is not on the page.
- The copy uses phrases the user or their audience actually said. Do not replace them with smoother ones.
- Headline candidates are proposals. The user chooses; a choice you dislike is still their choice.
- Section order follows the argument for this offer, not the blueprint. Drop and reorder with a reason.
- Never add urgency, guarantees, discounts or bonuses the user did not state.
