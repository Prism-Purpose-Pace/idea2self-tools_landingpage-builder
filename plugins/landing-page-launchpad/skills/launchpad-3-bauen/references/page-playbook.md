# Page playbook

How to turn the strategy into a Seitenkonzept. These are working defaults from conversion practice; most published evidence is single case studies, so quote no numbers for them and let the user's knowledge of their audience overrule a default when they give a reason.

## 1. Principles

- One page, one goal, one action. A section either moves the visitor toward the action or answers an objection. Otherwise it is cut.
- The first screen answers without scrolling: what is this, is it for me, what do I do next.
- The headline confirms what the visitor saw right before clicking (strategy: letzter Kontaktpunkt).
- Phone first. Links from messengers and social media are mostly opened on phones.
- Specific beats impressive: a situation, a date, a named result.
- Ask for as little data as the next step needs.
- A short page for a simple offer. Length follows the number of real objections, not a template.

## 2. Argument line and required parts

Write the argument line before any section: five sentences, each from the strategy.

1. **Wer:** who this is for, in their situation.
2. **Spannung:** what bothers them, in their words.
3. **Versprechen:** what changes with the offer.
4. **Beweis:** why to believe it (strongest honest proof).
5. **Handlung:** the one next step and what happens after it.

Five parts are required on every page: first screen, problem, offer, trust, action. Everything else is added only when real content exists and an objection calls for it: process or agenda, for whom and for whom not, details and conditions, questions and answers. Do not add a section because landing pages usually have it.

Section headings are statements, not labels: „Nach zwei Stunden läuft deine erste Abrechnung", not „Unsere Lösung".

The same action, with the same wording, appears on the first screen and at the end; on long pages once in between. One primary button style. No navigation that leads away; footer links only for Impressum and Datenschutz.

## 3. Starting structures by kind of action

Starting points, to be reordered by the argument line.

| Action | Usual order | What decides |
|---|---|---|
| Event | First screen with name, date, place, price · for whom · what you take home · agenda · host · practical details · questions · action | Date, place and price visible at once; what participants leave with |
| Warteliste / Launch | First screen · problem · what is coming and when · why sign up now (only real reasons) · who is behind it · action | Honesty about what exists today |
| Newsletter | First screen · what arrives, how often, for whom · one sample or topic list · who writes · action | A concrete sample beats a description |
| Erstgespräch | First screen · problem · how the conversation runs and what it costs · who you talk to · what happens afterwards · action | Removing fear of a sales call |
| Info-Material | First screen with what is inside · for whom · three things you will know afterwards · who wrote it · action | Naming the content precisely |

Form fields by default: first name and e-mail; e-mail only for newsletter, waitlist and material; add a field only if the user named it as necessary. Button says what happens: „Platz sichern", „Auf die Warteliste", „Termin anfragen". The line under the button answers the next worry, if true: cost, what happens to the data, no obligation.

## 4. From strategy to page

| Strategy | Used in |
|---|---|
| Positionierungssatz | headline and subline |
| Typische Situation, `[Zitat Zielgruppe]` | problem section, wording everywhere |
| Bisherige Lösungen | end of the problem section, questions |
| Angebot, Unterschied | offer section, subline |
| „Was auf die Seite darf" | trust section, every factual sentence |
| Handlung, Eckdaten | first screen, form, message after sign-up |
| Akzeptierte Risiken, real hesitations the user has heard | questions and answers |
| Hauptkanal, letzter Kontaktpunkt | headline wording, announcement text, link preview |

Questions and answers come from hesitations real people have voiced, as far as the user knows them, and from the audience's other challenges. Roast findings are internal and are not turned into questions by themselves.

## 5. Texts

- Use the audience's words, not the provider's vocabulary.
- Headline: the result or relief for the visitor in about ten words. For an event, name and date belong on the first screen even when the headline is a promise.
- Offer three headline candidates from different angles, not three versions of one: result, situation the reader recognises, stance of the provider, a question the audience asks itself, the offer named plainly, a contrast with the usual way.
- One form of address throughout. Short sentences, active verbs, at most three lines per paragraph on a phone.
- Numbers, dates and prices exactly as confirmed.
- Follow the four kinds of sentences in the core rules. A founder without proof can still write strong scenes, commitments and a stance; what is not allowed is a number, a result or a guarantee without basis.
- No invented urgency.
- If the audience or the user uses gender-inclusive wording, ask once which form the page should use and keep to it.

Phrases that mark German copy as machine-written. Rewrite any sentence that contains them, and do not replace them with the nearest synonym; say the concrete thing instead:

- openers like „Stell dir vor, …", „In der heutigen Zeit …", „Kennst du das?"
- „Nicht nur …, sondern auch …" in series; „Kein X. Kein Y. Nur Z."
- adjectives in threes; „innovativ", „ganzheitlich", „maßgeschneidert", „nahtlos", „auf das nächste Level"
- empty imperatives: „Entdecke", „Tauche ein", „Starte durch"
- emoji as bullets, exclamation marks in headings, a closing line that repeats the paragraph as a slogan

## 6. Proof without invention

Use the strongest that honestly exists:

1. named quote with permission and a specific result
2. own numbers with their basis („12 Teilnehmende beim ersten Durchgang")
3. relevant experience and qualifications, stated plainly
4. the provider's own story with the problem, as personal experience
5. openness that this is new, together with a low-risk offer

A first-time offer uses 3 to 5. If proof is missing, list it in the Seitenkonzept under „Fehlt noch" instead of filling the gap.

## 7. Three text tests

Run them on your draft before the user sees it, and offer the first one to the user.

- **Vorlese-Test:** read the first screen aloud. Would the provider say this sentence to a person across the table?
- **Austausch-Test:** replace the offer's name with a competitor's. If the text still fits, it says nothing specific.
- **Überschriften-Test:** read only the headings from top to bottom. Do they tell the argument line on their own?

## 8. Examples

From an unrelated field; patterns only.

| Element | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | „Willkommen bei Praxisglück" | „Feierabend um sechs, auch am Monatsende" |
| Subline | „Innovative Lösungen für Ihren Erfolg" | „Für Physiotherapie-Praxen mit bis zu fünf Angestellten. Ich richte alles bei dir am Empfang ein." |
| Problem | „Praxen stehen vor immer größeren Herausforderungen." | „Es ist der 28., kurz vor acht. Die letzte Patientin ist weg, der Stapel Verordnungen liegt noch da." |
| Zusage | „Garantiert 50 % weniger Aufwand" | „Ich bleibe, bis deine erste Abrechnung durchgelaufen ist." |
| Beweis | „Hunderte zufriedene Kunden" | „Erster Durchgang im Mai mit 8 Praxen; 6 arbeiten weiter damit." |
| Frage | „Warum sind wir die beste Wahl?" | „Was, wenn ich am Termin doch nicht kann?" |
